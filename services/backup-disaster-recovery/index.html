Backup, Disaster Recovery & BCDR

Backup monitoring, restore planning and business-continuity preparation for servers, endpoints, cloud workloads and critical data.

When this helps

Common reasons organizations call us

  • Backups run but nobody knows whether restores will work
  • Ransomware or data-loss risk requires a stronger recovery plan
  • Critical systems have unclear recovery priorities
  • A server or application failure would stop operations
  • Cloud/SaaS data protection assumptions are unclear
  • The business needs a documented recovery path before an incident

What the service can include

  • Backup coverage and ownership review
  • Server/workload backup configuration support by platform
  • Cloud/SaaS backup planning by scope
  • Retention and storage review
  • Backup-alert monitoring by agreement
  • Restore testing and validation by scope
  • Recovery priority and dependency mapping
  • Disaster-recovery runbook development
  • Business-continuity technology planning
  • Recovery-time/recovery-point discussion and documentation
  • Offsite/immutable protection considerations by platform
  • Post-incident restoration coordination

Design from recovery backwards

We start with what must be recoverable, in what order, and by whom. Backup jobs, retention and storage choices are then evaluated against those recovery expectations.

What is documented

Protected workloads, retention, alert ownership, restoration procedures, recovery priorities and test results are documented at the level appropriate to the engagement.

Frequently asked questions

Useful scope questions before a project begins.

Is backup the same as disaster recovery?

No. Backup preserves data; disaster recovery defines how systems and operations are restored after a major failure.

Do Microsoft 365 or other SaaS platforms eliminate the need for backup planning?

No. Native retention and resilience do not automatically answer every recovery, retention or business-continuity requirement.

Can recovery be guaranteed?

No responsible provider should promise recovery without understanding the platform, failure mode, dependencies and tested restore process.
