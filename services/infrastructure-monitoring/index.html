Infrastructure Monitoring & Management

Proactive visibility and operational management for supported servers, network devices, services and infrastructure components.

When this helps

Common reasons organizations call us

  • Problems are discovered only after users complain
  • Critical devices or services have no health visibility
  • Capacity issues are not noticed until performance degrades
  • Multiple sites need consistent operational checks
  • Infrastructure alerts exist but nobody owns them
  • A growing environment needs more structured maintenance

What the service can include

  • Server/service health monitoring by platform
  • Network-device monitoring by scope
  • Availability and resource-threshold alerting
  • RMM/monitoring policy configuration
  • Patch and maintenance coordination
  • Event/log review by scope
  • Capacity and storage visibility
  • Alert ownership and escalation workflows
  • Site and device inventory alignment
  • Recurring maintenance checks
  • Documentation of monitored assets and thresholds
  • Integration with ticketing/operational workflows where supported

How Orbiwest approaches the work

We map the current environment, dependencies, ownership and business impact before making changes. The goal is a supportable result with realistic sequencing, security, documentation and rollback where appropriate.

What is documented

Material configurations, decisions, dependencies, handoff information and agreed operating responsibilities are documented at a level appropriate to the engagement.

Good fit when you need

  • Clear technical ownership
  • Practical planning before change
  • Maintainable implementation
  • Security considered during design
  • Documentation and handoff

Frequently asked questions

Useful scope questions before a project begins.

Does monitoring mean 24/7 human response?

Not automatically. Monitoring and response coverage depend on the service agreement and tools selected.

Can you monitor network equipment?

Yes, by scope and platform, using supported management/monitoring methods.

What happens to alerts?

Useful alerts should have defined ownership, severity and escalation rather than simply generating email noise.
